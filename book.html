Practices for the Road

The practices from the book, each a few minutes long. Press Watch to follow a short guided video, or read the steps and try one right now.

1Guided Video

Five, Four, Three, Two, One

When your body is still racing and you need to come back to right here. From Grounded in Coffee.

  • Name five things you can see.
  • Four things you can feel.
  • Three things you can hear.
  • Two things you can smell.
  • One thing you can taste.
  • Take your time with each one. By the end, you are back in the room.
2Guided Video

Four, Seven, Eight Breathing

When a heavy moment follows you out the door.

  • Breathe in quietly through your nose for a count of four.
  • Hold it gently for a count of seven.
  • Breathe out slowly and completely through your mouth for a count of eight.
  • Do it four times. Let each long breath out loosen what you are holding.

3Guided Video

Lion’s Breath

When the day starts sideways and you need to shake it off fast.

  • Take a deep breath in through your nose.
  • Open your mouth wide, stick your tongue out as far as it will go, and breathe out with a long, loud “haaaa.”
  • Add a little roar if you want. Three rounds. Laughing at yourself counts.
4Guided Video

Name It

When your heart begins to tighten.

  • Pause. Name the feeling softly inside: “This is grief.” “This is fear.” “This is love.”
  • Let the words make a little breathing room. Then feel your feet on the floor and the breath moving in and out.

“Naming the fatigue was the first gentle step back toward wholeness.”

5Guided Video

Listening Beneath the Words

For the conversations that matter most.

  • Pause before you answer. Take one full breath and ask yourself what you are sensing beneath the words.
  • Say back what you notice: “It sounds like this is weighing heavily on you.”
  • Name what is in the room, with kindness: “There is a lot of love here.”
  • Or ask the question that opened a room for me: “What is this moment asking of you right now?”
6Guided Video

Whose Is This?

When you are carrying more than is yours. From I Can Use Both.

  • Sit somewhere quiet, two hands on something steady. Three slow breaths, longer on the way out.
  • Then sort, out loud, like unpacking a backpack after a long trip. Name what belongs to someone else: “That’s theirs.” Then name what is yours, and keep only that.

7Written Practice

Leave It Here

At the end of a long day, before you walk in your own door.

  • Mark the change with something small: a few deep breaths in the car, a favorite song, a change of clothes at the door.
  • Say it plainly, out loud or in a journal: “Today was heavy.”
  • Then the words that let it rest: “I have done enough. I am enough.”
8Guided Video

Saying What Matters

For the people you love, while there is time.

  • Four simple things that matter most: “Please forgive me.” “I forgive you.” “Thank you.” “I love you.”
  • Say the ones that are yours to say, in your own words, out loud or in a letter.

9Written Practice

One Moment of Beauty

To close the day.

  • Before you sleep, recall one moment of beauty you witnessed today. A faint smile. A tender touch. A song in a school gym.

“Let that light be the last thing you carry home.”

Practices for the Road, from In the End: What Sitting with the Dying Taught Me about Living, by Chris Joy.

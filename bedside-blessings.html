Farewells

Bedside Blessings

Prayers, blessings, and quiet rituals for the last days, at home, in hospice, or in the hospital.

Ask About a Bedside Blessing

Near the end of life, families often want to mark what is happening: to say goodbye, to give thanks, to bless the person they love. A bedside blessing gives that moment shape.

Chris has served as a hospice, ICU, and pediatric chaplain. We come to the bedside, follow the family's lead, and offer prayers, readings, songs, or simple rituals that fit your loved one and your family.

What to expect

From first hello to the day itself.

  1. 1

    Reach out

    Call or write. If time is short, mark it URGENT.

  2. 2

    A few questions

    We learn about your loved one, your family, and what feels right.

  3. 3

    The blessing

    A gentle gathering at the bedside, usually 20 to 40 minutes.

  4. 4

    Afterward

    We stay as long as needed, and check in with the family later.

Make it yours

Ways we can shape it

  • Prayers and readings from your tradition, or words that fit you
  • Anointing or laying on of hands if that is part of your faith
  • Words of thanks, forgiveness, and goodbye
  • Music or singing
  • Children included in gentle, honest ways
Good to know

Practical details

  • We work alongside hospice and hospital teams, never in place of them.
  • Available at home, in facilities, and in hospitals.
  • We also serve families in pediatrics and the NICU.
Common questions
Is this last rites?

It can include prayers from your tradition. For sacraments that require your own clergy, we are glad to help you reach them.

What if my loved one cannot respond?

Hearing may remain late in life. We speak to them directly and include them in everything.

How soon can you come?

When a death has just happened or is close, mark your message urgent, and we'll usually reach out the same day. We gladly work alongside your funeral home and hospice team.

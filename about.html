How we work

What you can count on.

  • Chaplaincy, not therapy or medical care. We offer spiritual guidance, support, and presence, and we help you find clinical care when it would help.
  • Open to all faith traditions and everything in-between. We talk openly about faith when it helps you, and your beliefs lead.
  • Privacy first. Our tools keep your answers on your device, and what you share with us stays with us, except where the law or your safety requires otherwise.
  • Grounded in current research. Our tools are built from current research and years of real chaplaincy work.
  • Independent. Grow With Grounded is our own practice. It is separate from any hospice, hospital, or church.
  • Everyone is welcome, whatever your budget. If money is tight, tell us, and we'll find a way.
Why a tree

Six parts make you whole.

Every tree has roots, a trunk, bark, branches, leaves, and fruit, and so do people: what grounds you, your purpose, your mind and feelings, your relationships, your body, and your hope. Being whole means noticing and tending all six. Everything we do, from a wedding to a bedside vigil to a check-in for a fourth grader, starts there.

Leadership

Kayti and Chris Joy.

Married 22 years. Four boys. We've sat on both sides of the bed.

We've spent our working lives showing up for people. Chris as a chaplain in the ICU, on a behavioral health unit, through the COVID pandemic, and now in hospice. Kayti in classrooms and yoga studios, and for seven years as a caregiver for her family at the end of life.

Raising four boys taught us the rest: life's biggest thresholds, the vows, the new babies, the hard diagnoses, the last goodbyes, are easier to cross together. Grow With Grounded is how we keep showing up: guidance and support for life's thresholds, tools for resilience, and stories from the edge of life.

What we believe

What we believe about this work.

  • Presence matters more than answers. Most of the time, the most helpful thing is to stay.
  • You are the expert on your own life. Your faith, your doubts, your family, your way.
  • Dying is still living. The last chapter deserves honesty, care, and even laughter.
  • Grief keeps its own schedule. It continues long after the funeral, and it asks to be honored rather than fixed.
  • Caregivers need care too. The people holding everyone else together deserve someone in their corner.

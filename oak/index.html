Have you ever known a person, or perhaps been a person, who gives the impression that everything is good, but you sense it would only take one small bump in the road before the house of cards comes tumbling down? It takes one to know one. I lived this way for a long time, and I have met countless others who have felt the same.

The answer is resilience: your ability to bounce back when something unexpected and unwanted occurs. The key is strengthening the different parts of your life, so when one part is injured, the healthy parts provide support for real healing and a return to wholeness.

A person is a lot like a tree. A healthy tree is healthy because all its parts grow together. It needs roots that hold, a trunk that carries, bark that heals, branches that reach, leaves that breathe, and fruit that carries the future. Oak looks at all six parts of you, from root to fruit.

The six parts of your tree
How Oak works

Take a check-in: eight short questions for each part, about the past two weeks, or a Quick Check-in in about 2 minutes. Your answers grow a tree and a growth plan with practices that fit your life. Check them off each day in Today, take a short check-in each week, and a full check-in every twelve weeks adds a ring. Learn has short videos for every part of your tree. In Settings, Health and Ability fits the practices to your body and your days, with a private Living Sober choice inside it. Read Aloud and Save or Print are on every guide and plan, and Back Up Everything keeps it all safe. Everything stays on this device, locked in your own profile.

When Life Changes

Guides for more than 80 hard seasons of life, from grief and diagnosis to burnout, betrayal, and doubt. Each guide has a view for when you are going through it, a view for when you are helping someone else, and two short videos. Open When Life Changes

Every age has its own tree: Maple, Aspen, and Pine for kids and teens, Birch for young adults, Sequoia for 60 and up, Willow for hospice, and The Grove for a family or group growing together.

One Rule

Name each resource in only one part of your tree. If your family gives you hope, they still belong in the Branches, not the Fruit. This constraint reveals your true resources in each part, and where you may be leaning on one part of the tree to carry weight it was never meant to hold alone.

Your Growth Plan

Start with about 3 practices for each part of your tree, and a few more for each growing edge, or as many as you like. They show up in Today, ready to check off, and you can change them anytime. This is the practical next step that turns your check-in into real growth.
